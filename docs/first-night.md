# First-night guide: one sharp photograph of M42

Dates, times and exposure numbers for the first session are in
[`session-plan.md`](session-plan.md) (generated by `startracker plan`). All numbers there are
predictions. This page is the procedure.

## Pack list

Tracker, sturdy tripod, both ball heads, charged 12 V pack, camera with **two charged batteries**
(cold nights and long exposures drain them), lens (200 mm or the kit zoom), Bahtinov mask, the
E3 remote cable, phone (web UI), red torch, lens cloth, a dew strap or hand warmer for the lens,
warm clothes. Allow 90 minutes before the imaging window.

## 1. Set up (T-90 min)

1. Firm ground, out of the wind and away from lights. Level the tripod roughly.
2. Sturdy ball head on the tripod, tracker on the ball head. The **tower end of the hinge points
   north and up**: looking north along the hinge, the top board sits anticlockwise of the bottom
   board, so opening the boards follows the sky. (With the boards level, the drive end points east.)
3. Power on. Join the `StarTracker-XXXX` Wi-Fi and open `http://192.168.4.1`. The page sends
   your phone's clock, which the Polaris aid needs. Red display, phone at minimum brightness.
4. `Home` (or hold the BOOT button for 2 s). Wait for "idle, homed".

## 2. Polar alignment (T-75 min)

1. Tilt the tracker so the hinge axis points about 33.9 deg above the northern horizon (a phone
   inclinometer on the sight tube gets you within a degree).
2. The page's **Polar alignment** card shows a clock face: where Polaris must sit on the
   reticle ring, e.g. "Polaris on the ring at 10:40 o'clock". Up means towards the zenith.
3. Look through the eyepiece cap (south end). Find Polaris inside the aperture, then move the
   ball head (azimuth: turn it; altitude: tilt it) until Polaris touches the edge of the
   aperture at that clock position.
4. Lock the head, look again: locking often shifts it. Re-check after 15 minutes; the clock
   position turns by half a clock-hour per hour.

Predicted accuracy of this method: about 0.15-0.4 deg (the 1.5 mm pinhole limits it). That
supports the planned 20 s subs at 200 mm; the model says the screw's periodic error, not the
alignment, limits longer subs (README section 6).

## 3. Camera and focus (T-50 min)

1. Camera on the small ball head. Manual mode, RAW, image stabilisation off, autofocus off, long
   exposure noise reduction off, high ISO noise reduction off, white balance daylight, BULB.
2. Plug in the E3 cable.
3. Bahtinov mask on the lens, live view on the brightest star near Orion (Betelgeuse, Rigel or
   Sirius), zoom x10. Turn the focus ring until the middle spike of the pattern sits exactly
   between the other two. Tape the focus ring. Remove the mask.

## 4. Frame and test (T-30 min)

1. Find Orion's belt; M42 is the fuzzy middle "star" of the sword below it. At 200 mm the frame
   is 6.4 x 4.3 deg; put M42 slightly off centre with the Running Man nebula (just north) in
   the frame.
2. `Test 5 s` at ISO 1600 to check framing.
3. Start tracking (`Sidereal`), then take one full-length test sub (e.g. `test 20`).
4. Zoom to 100 % on the camera screen:
   - Round stars: proceed.
   - Stars trailed **east-west** (along the direction the sky moves): drive error. Check the
     preload springs, then try a shorter sub.
   - Stars trailed **north-south**: polar alignment. Redo step 2.
   - Stars doubled or smeared in random directions: wind or a loose ball head.
5. Histogram: the sky peak in the left third. Lower the ISO if it is further right.

## 5. Shoot (T)

1. `Rewind` so the run gets the full ~2.3 h of travel (predicted), re-frame if needed, `Sidereal`.
2. Camera card: frames, exposure, gap 3 s, delay 5 s. `Start`. The page counts frames and shows
   the tracking time left.
3. Before the travel runs out (the sequence stops itself if it does), stop, `Rewind` (~40 s),
   re-frame on M42, start again.
4. Optional HDR: 20 x 3 s at the same ISO for the Trapezium core.

## 6. Calibration frames (end of session)

- **Darks:** cap on, same ISO, same exposure, 20 frames, at the end while the camera is at night
  temperature: `shoot 20 20`.
- **Flats:** before touching focus. Lens covered with a white T-shirt, pointed at the dawn sky (or
  a tablet showing white), Av mode, 30 frames.
- **Bias:** cap on, fastest shutter, 50 frames.

## 7. Stack (free software)

[Siril](https://siril.org) (Linux, Windows, macOS):

1. New project; put lights, darks, flats and bias in their own folders.
2. Scripts -> `OSC_Preprocessing` (one-shot colour): calibrates, debayers, registers, stacks
   with sigma clipping.
3. Image -> Background extraction, then Photometric colour calibration.
4. Stretch with the histogram transformation (or Generalised Hyperbolic Stretch), gently.
5. Save as 16-bit TIFF for final touches.

DeepSkyStacker (Windows) works equally well for the stacking step; its frame scores help reject
trailed subs automatically.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Boards close when tracking | motor direction | swap one coil pair or set `INVERT_DIR` |
| `home_not_found` | switch not reached / unplugged | check the switch wiring (NC) and the flag |
| `switch_stuck` | switch pressed after backing off | lever bent or wired NO instead of NC |
| Trails grow over the night, all frames | polar alignment | re-align; field rotation shows at the corners first |
| Periodic soft frames every ~2 min | screw periodic error (predicted ~137 s period) | measure and load PEC (build guide section 10) |
| Web page unreachable | phone left the tracker's Wi-Fi | rejoin `StarTracker-XXXX`; serial console works too |
| Camera does not fire | not in BULB / cable in the wrong port | BULB mode, E3 port, try `test 2` |
